Why do things float

Discover how unseen forces allow origami boats and massive cruise ships to float effortlessly across the water.

Step 01

The Everyday Mystery

If you toss a pebble into a lake, it goes directly to the bottom in seconds. Yet, a big cruise of thousands of tons floats effortlessly on the exact same water.

Our common intuition tells us that heavy things sink and light ones float, but I guess we've just seen an example that contradicts this. So, why not tell the full story of what actually happens with things on water.

Step 02

The Force that Pushes Back

Key Concepts

Archimedes' Principle

It states that any object that is fully or partially submerged in a fluid (e.g. water) will experience an upward buoyant force which is equal to the weight of the fluid displaced by the object.

Newton's Third Law

It states that whenever one object exerts a force on another object, the second object simultaneously exerts a force of equal magnitude and opposite direction on the first object.

Buoyant Force

The actual upward force exerted by a fluid on a submerged or partially submerged object. Its magnitude is determined by Achimedes' Principle.

So what actually happens with the force is that it creates a constant battle between two opposing forces:

  • Gravity: Pulls the object downward (based on the object's mass).
  • Buoyant Force: Pushes the object upward (equal to the weight of displaced water).

If the buoyant force is greater than the object's weight, it accelerates upwards. If its weight is greater, it sinks. When both forces are equal, the object is in vertical equilibrium and can float or remain suspended in the fluid.

Step 03

Real World Applications

Engineers harness buoyancy every single day to accomplish things that seem to defy gravity:

🚢

Steel Ships

Hollow hulls trap large amounts of air inside, making the ship's average density far less than water.

🤿

Submarines

Submarines fill their ballast tanks with water to increase their average density and dive. To rise, they use compressed air to expel water from the tanks, decreasing their average density.

🧊

Icebergs

Ice is about 90% as dense as seawater, which is why 90% of an iceberg stays hidden beneath the surface.
