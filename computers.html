How come my calls sound so good

Discover how softwares analyse your sound waves and make their science to make your voice sound just fine.

Step 01

The Everyday Mystery

Imagine you're attending an important online meeting from a busy café. People are talking, cars are passing outside, and someone is typing on a keyboard nearby. Despite all these chaotic sounds, the person on the other side of the call can hear your voice clearly. But how?

Your microphone captures more than just your voice. It also captures the surrounding sounds. So, how does your device distinguish what you want people to hear from everything else? The answer lies in the physics of sound waves and modern audio processing technology.

Step 02

The Science Behind Your Voice

Key Concepts

Sound Waves

These are mechanical vibrations in air that carry sound. These have the characteristics of frequency and amplitude.

Frequency

How many times does a wave oscillate per second. As a matter of fact, frequency is directly proportional to pitch, so higher frequency means higher pitch and vice versa.

Amplitude

Maximum variation of a wave from its equilibrium position. In spund waves, it can describe the maximum variation in air pressure. Greater amplitude generally corresponds to greater perceived loudness, assuming others conditions remain comparable.

SNR (Signal to Noise Ratio)

Measures how strong is the voice in comparison to other sounds. It is represented in dB.

The Technologies That Make it Posible

So now that we understand the physics behind sound, we can explore how devices use it.

  • Noise Suppression: Reduces unwanted background sounds, such as traffic, keyboard typing and environmental noise, while attempting to preserve the speaker's voice.
    1. Audio Input: The microphone captures sound and converts it into digital audio data.
    2. Analysis: The system analyzes the audio, often dividing it into smaller frames and examining their frequency components.
    3. Noise Detection: An algorithm estimates which parts of the audio are likely to be unwanted background noise.
    4. Filtering: The system reduces the estimated noise while attempting to preserve the speech signal.
    5. Output: The processed audio is reconstructed, producing a clearer voice.
  • Voice Isolation:Uses audio processing techniques and machine learning to distinguish the target voice from surrounding sounds and prioritize it over background noise.
    1. Audio Input: The microphone captures the target speaker's voice along with other sounds.
    2. Feature Extraction: The system analyzes characteristics such as frequency patterns, timing and speech variations.
    3. Voice Recognition: A trained machine learning model estimates which parts of the audio belong to the target voice.
    4. Separation: The algorithm separates the estimated voice from surrounding sounds, reducing unwanted audio.
    5. Output: The system produces audio in which the target speaker's voice is more prominent.

To sum up, your device receives a mixture of sound waves: your voice and background noise. Therefore, audio processing algorithms analyze this mixture and estimate which components belong to the desired voice. They then reduce unwanted sounds while preserving as much speech information as possible. This improves the effective signal-to-noise ratio.

Real World Applications

Engineers use these principles to improve communication in environments where background noise can interfere with speech:

✈️

Aviation Headsets

Help pilots communicate with air traffic control despite engine noise and other sounds inside the cockpit.

💻

Video Conferencing

Technologies such as noise suppression help make conversations clearer during online meetings, even in noisy environments.

🎤

Recording Studios

Audio engineers use microphones, filtering and digital signal processing to isolate vocals and reduce unwanted sounds.
